Works Cited

Master bibliography and source register for the R Theory website and research program.

Purpose. This page records external sources used to support, contextualize, test, or challenge claims published in R Theory. It is the shared destination for literature matches identified during research reviews, including searches conducted with Consensus.

Editorial standard. A source is not evidence for a claim merely because it is related to the topic. Each entry should identify the work precisely, provide a stable link or identifier, state what it is being used for, and distinguish direct support from background context or disagreement.

Literature matches from Consensus

New matches will be added here only after the paper itself has been checked. Each record should include:

  1. Full citation: authors, title, publication or repository, year, and DOI or stable URL.
  2. Relevant R Theory topic, theorem, definition, or empirical claim.
  3. Relationship: supporting result, related formulation, background, competing account, or contradiction.
  4. Evidence locator: page, section, theorem, equation, figure, or quoted result where applicable.
  5. Assessment: what the source establishes, what it does not establish, and any limitations.

Citation and review policy

  • Preserve the source's actual claim; do not strengthen it in paraphrase.
  • Prefer original papers, authoritative datasets, standards, and primary documentation over tertiary summaries.
  • Record disagreements and negative results as carefully as supporting results.
  • Do not cite a Consensus-generated summary as a substitute for the underlying publication.
  • Keep literature assessment separate from R Theory's own proofs, assertions, imports, and open questions.
  • When a source is relevant to a specific page or book, link the citation back to that location.
